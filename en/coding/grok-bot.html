Permissions & limits

Verification supplemented 2026-10-03; the nature of that spec is discussed in the body section "How to read that governance spec".

Three points verified on the launch pages:

How to read that governance spec (important — do not mistake it for product mechanics)

There is a public Bot behavior spec on the x.ai domain (x.ai/bot/…) containing a fairly complete approval list.

It fills the approval granularity missing from the launch pages, but its nature must be made clear.

Content
What it isA behavioral-constraint statement for one Bot — how it should act, what it should ask first
What it ❌ is notNot product documentation; not a product-level permission-mechanism statement; it does not mean all Bots are bound by the same ruleset; it cannot answer "can rules be configured in the user UI"

Why this distinction matters:

It tells you "how this Bot is required to behave", not "what this product lets you configure".

What users really want to know is "can I decide myself which actions need approval" — this document does not answer that question, so permissions still cannot count as filled.

But two of its designs deserve separate note, and they form interesting contrasts with the other two vendors:

  1. "Do not infer approval from silence" — silence is not consent. This is stricter than Dot's two tiers of "Ask for some actions / Always ask": xAI explicitly forbids inferring consent from "not objecting", at the spec level.
  2. "Approval applies only to the exact action, target, scope, version, configuration, connection, permission, and schedule" — approval is pinned to the single action and single configuration; it does not spill into the next time. This contrasts with Muse's granularity options:
  • Allow once / for this task / for this site / Always allow

These are two different granularity philosophies: Muse offers validity-duration options, while this Grok Bot spec recognizes one-time approval only.

The 1.0 update (11 November 2026)

xAI moved Grok Bot to 1.0 on 11 November 2026. The official heading says "Bots now work with your whole team, take on work without being asked, and get more done, faster". The update turns the product from a single-person always-on Bot into a team-shared Bot:

  • Team Bots: give a Bot files, apps and domain knowledge, then share it with the team so everyone starts from the same context; the Team Bot keeps learning while it collaborates.
  • Proactive work: 1.0 explicitly says "take on work without being asked", rather than only waiting for a user command. This is the same line as "keep working 24/7" on the launch page.
  • More job-shaped launches: "Grok Bot for Accounting" on 16 November 2026 is a post-1.0 accounting-team use case, showing the vendor sells by job rather than by feature.
  • Still no model name: neither the 1.0 page nor the launch page says which Grok model the Bot runs on; this dossier continues not to infer.

Selection impact: if you want a team-shared, always-on workspace, 1.0 is more worth a look than the launch version. If you need an auditable product-level permission configuration, 1.0 still does not give a complete answer, and that remains this entry's biggest shortfall.
