Collection · data annotation

Data annotation: fix the evaluation before the format

Annotation is not “finish drawing the boxes”. First write which evaluation or training task the data feeds, then decide what to label, who reviews and how disagreements are resolved. Image, video, 3D and text use different tools, but the quality process is the same: guideline, pre-label, sampling, arbitration and versioning.

This is a “who owns which layer” collection, not a duplicate of product dossiers; open an entry for its eight axes, sources and boundaries.

Which layer does what

LayerExisting entries

Commercial · self-hostedProdigy: scriptable annotation with a model in the loop; $390 personal lifetime / $490 per seat in five-seat packs
Text and preference dataArgilla: text annotation, review and dataset versions
Evaluation and dataset cardsDataset cards: source, licence, commercial use and quality caveats
Benchmark cards: what is measured, scoring, contamination, reproduction

Minimum working order

  1. Write a 10-item guideline and 10 boundary examples before batch labelling
  2. Use pre-labelling plus human review; fix the sampling rate and arbitration rules first
  3. Bind dataset version, guideline version and model evaluation version together

Boundary

The sites have not compared annotation tools on throughput or inter-annotator agreement; quality depends on the guideline, training and arbitration, not the brand. Prodigy is closed-source and commercial: as of November 2026 its official page lists $390 for a personal lifetime licence and $490 per seat in five-seat packs, excluding tax. For personal data, biometrics or sensitive content, authorisation, de-identification and retention need separate handling.
