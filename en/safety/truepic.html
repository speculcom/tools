It verifies whether a photo was taken on the spot, not whether a sentence may be published. The official docs describe capture-time verification: a PKI signing certificate plus C2PA content credentials, with on-site consistency checks for time, location, screen recapture and sharpness.

When to use it, when not to

Fit: insurance claims, loan collateral, equipment inspection and other evidence-chain workflows, and teams wiring a verification verdict into their own systems through the API. Not fit: LLM input/output filtering (that is the guardrail line), or self-serve signup and trial; the official getting-started page says there is no self-serve onboarding, only demo and contract.

Model & openness

No model picking: the recorded detection surface is Truepic's own authentication and inspection pipeline. The verification-tests page lists blur, time accuracy, location, screen-recapture detection and optional AI tasks; the Lens side judges provenance through PKI-signed certificates and C2PA content credentials (Lens API page on record). Whether the underlying models are disclosed or replaceable is not stated item by item and is recorded as unknown.

Where it runs

Delivered as a hosted cloud: the Vision web platform (login entry and Okta SSO are in the docs) plus a REST API. The regions page records only US and UK, and states data is stored in the region where the API request is made. No official statement of a local or offline deployment form was found.

Local files

You upload images, video and inspection records; results come back as a JSON report, and inspections can be exported as PDFs (that page sits in the documentation navigation). Lens Web SDK captures on the browser side. Nothing is recorded about reading your local filesystem, nor where media ultimately lands in storage.

Tasks after shutdown

Inspections move through task states: the Inspection Statuses, Alerts and Webhooks pages sit in the official documentation navigation, which is the shape of 'submit, get a conclusion asynchronously, receive callbacks'. Queue duration and retry details were not verified page by page and are recorded as unknown.

Tools & extensions

The extension surface is the API, not plugins: OAuth 2.0 authentication, the Lens API and Lens Web SDK (each with its own changelog), webhooks and webhook actions, website forms, and a webview embedded into native apps - all listed in the documentation navigation. No MCP or other tool-protocol statement was found; recorded as unknown.

Context & memory

The unit of organisation is a single inspection: Teams, Custom Fields, Lists and Message Template Sets are on record, and the Risk Network adds cross-customer history. This is not conversational context, and no cross-request memory mechanism is described; recorded as unknown.

Permissions & limits

The getting-started page records 'Vision does not provide self-serve signup'; access runs through demos and contracts. The API overview page records a limit of up to 20 requests per 10-second period. The privacy policy (effective 2026-01-23) records collection of identifiers, content and device data; model training on anonymised data; global sharing with sub-processors; retention per contract and 90 days after termination. Certification detail was not verified - the official trust page returned only a dynamic script shell - so it is recorded as unknown.

Best for

Fits: insurance claims, lending and collateral, equipment inspection - any workflow needing authenticated-at-capture evidence plus a defensible record - and teams wiring authentication verdicts into their own systems through the API. Not a fit: teams looking for LLM input/output filtering (not the product's main axis), anyone needing self-hosting, or anyone expecting a free self-serve quota (the pricing page records no free plan).

Commercial service under its own terms and privacy policy (legal and privacy-policy pages direct-fetched 2026-11-06; the privacy policy is effective 2026-01-23). No customer-facing open-source licence is on record, and no free self-serve tier is stated.

Test notes

This site has not completed measured runs; nothing here covers authentication accuracy, false-positive rates or latency.

Suggested measurements (three):

  • Whether the individual verification tests ever contradict each other on the same re-photographed image
  • Latency differences between the US and UK regions, and whether residency behaves as documented
  • How the report distinguishes 'credential missing' from 'credential damaged'

Capability boundaries

On record it can judge: whether media was captured live on a signing device, whether a screen was re-photographed, whether time and location are self-consistent, whether metadata was altered, plus optional AI analysis tasks. Not on record: it does not decide whether content is harmful, and it does not decide whether you should trust the picture. The C2PA boundary, in this site's view: credentials give provenance and processing history, not a final verdict on truthfulness (see the Content Credentials entry).
