An academic-literature "retrieve + structured extraction" workbench: research question → paper screening → extraction tables → cited synthesis — a systematic-review pipeline, AI-assisted; every cell still needs checking against the original.

When to use it, when not to

Systematic reviews and literature surveys (medical/social-science/ML English literature pools), researchers who want extraction tables rather than abstract-reading. Not for: Chinese literature as the main axis (the academic pool is English-facing), non-academic retrieval, full-text acquisition (it anchors on abstracts and available full-text snippets).

Model & openness

Academic retrieval system + LLM extraction pipeline: a self-built paper index (Semantic Scholar-family academic libraries as one underlying layer, official narrative) + multiple LLMs doing extraction/synthesis (official narrative, kept at category level).

Where it runs

Web (elicit.com) as the main entry; literature retrieval and extraction generation both happen on the vendor's cloud; no officially stated local form seen.

Local files

Projects/literature libraries stored on the account-side cloud; exports (CSV/review documents) count as local. No officially stated local data surface seen.

Tasks after shutdown

Retrieval is instant; systematic-review style tasks (multi-round screening/extraction tables) are a task line; no "keep-running-after-shutdown" concept.

Tools & extensions

Literature retrieval + structured extraction is the main axis: searching papers by research question, screening (inclusion/exclusion criteria surface), extraction tables (sample/method/conclusion columns), review synthesis (with citations); a "find similar papers" line (official product surface).

Context & memory

Permissions & limits

Account + subscription system (D9: pricing not collected); free-tier retrieval/extraction quota limits kept at the official category level; the training-use switch kept at the official category level.

Best for

Fits: systematic reviews/literature surveys (medicine/social sciences/ML and other English literature pools), researchers who want "extraction tables" rather than "reading abstracts". Does not fit: Chinese-literature-first (the academic libraries are English-leaning), non-academic retrieval, wanting full-text access (it anchors abstracts and available full-text snippets).

Top misconception

  • "AI reads papers for you" is not "AI conclusions are trustworthy" — every cell of the extraction table needs checking against the original, especially numbers
  • Retrieval coverage depends on the underlying academic database — papers outside it cannot be found; systematic reviews need multi-database cross-checks
  • elicit.com returns a Cloudflare 403 to curl — our verification method is limited and noted honestly; it is not a site problem
